# Timeline and viewing sphere

The timeline spans 30 UTC calendar days, from 00:00 on the start date through 23:59 on day 30. Live clock follows UTC when now is inside that range. Manual scrubbing pauses it.

The 3D viewing sphere defaults to 100 lunar distances (LD). Choose 25, 50 or 100 LD. At a shared epoch, each asteroid's JPL position is interpolated from its hourly position and velocity samples. The dot appears when its Euclidean distance from Earth's center is less than or equal to the chosen radius, and disappears when it moves outside. This uses current predicted distance, independent of the table's closest-approach filter.

One LD is 384,400 km. The sphere's radius is 7.8 scene units, so `km per scene unit = viewing radius in LD × 384400 / 7.8`. Time changes, loaded coordinates and asteroid selection do not resize this frame. Changing the viewing radius explicitly changes the scale. Earth and asteroid display sizes remain enlarged and cannot be used to infer physical collisions.

NASA NeoWs supplies 44 days of encounter candidates: the visible 30-day timeline and seven days before and after it, using seven serial requests of at most seven dates each. Metrics, timeline bars, table and CSV include only encounters in the visible 30 days. The 3D viewer also considers buffered encounters, deduplicates objects by NASA ID, and queues all candidates matching the filters. Source coverage is limited: objects outside this encounter catalog can be absent even while inside the sphere. This is not a complete solar-system inventory.

JPL Horizons supplies 60 days of hourly states starting at the timeline start. This supports every position in the 30-day scrubber and the selected object's next 30 days of predicted trajectory. Downloaded vectors are reused while scrubbing. Requests load serially, and catalog caches refresh after 15 minutes with the personal key (one hour with the demo key), while JPL vectors remain cached for one hour; both services back off on failures. Progress distinguishes visible dots from positioned candidates; missing vectors never become fabricated positions. Large candidate catalogs can take time to load. Newly discovered objects require a successful catalog refresh.

The selected path is drawn as independent sampled line segments clipped to the viewing sphere. Segment/sphere intersections determine displayed endpoints between hourly samples. Outside gaps are never bridged with false chords. This is a finite Earth-relative predicted track, not a closed orbit. No universal interpolation accuracy or uncertainty envelope is claimed.

In **View settings**, **Click again to close details** is enabled by default. Clicking an already selected marker or table item again clears the information and its path while keeping the side panel space fixed. Disable this preference to retain the selection on repeat clicks. The preference is saved in browser storage when available. Explicit Close and Clear selection controls also clear the selection. The side panel keeps its width even without a selection, preventing the model from jumping as information opens or closes. A selected object can move outside the sphere: its dot disappears while the panel still reports its distance and outside status.

The separate 2D mode remains an illustrative encounter schematic; its dots do not implement the 3D radius rule. If live catalog retrieval fails, the saved snapshot is labeled, and its actual dated timeline and candidate coverage are used.

While Live clock is enabled and the page is visible, the app checks once per minute whether the catalog refresh interval has elapsed. Background refresh preserves the selected object and scrub time for the same window. Likely nearby candidates are prioritized for downloads; the priority score never substitutes for JPL coordinates. IndexedDB stores successful vectors beyond localStorage's small string quota, with a one-hour source-timestamp expiry and graceful fallback if storage is unavailable. Existing marker meshes are reused, and decorative star redraws are limited to 20 frames per second without repeatedly projecting DOM marker labels.
